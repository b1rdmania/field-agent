## Negotiation notes

- **Minimum spend**: cited range for a 14-seat private room is £500–£2,500, with budget/off-peak rooms as low as £250–£500 and top-tier or weekend slots exceeding £5,000. Treat anything quoted well outside that band — high or low — as a flag to ask what's actually included.
- **Concrete anchor point**: CORD's Viceroy room is quoted at "from £1,500" for an afternoon session — use this as a reference figure when a venue won't give a number up front.
- **Room fee vs. spend**: Bentley's states no room hire fee at all, i.e. the whole cost is absorbed into food/drink minimum spend — worth asking every venue on this list whether they charge a separate hire fee on top of minimum spend, since that's the first thing to trade away.
- **Midweek/off-peak**: the cited answer is explicit that lower minimums are available on weekday slots or lunch versus peak evening — for a Thursday exec dinner specifically, push on this rather than accepting a rack rate built for Friday/Saturday.
- **Wine/corkage**: not addressed in the cited answers or venue text for this batch except that CORD includes an "extensive wine list" as part of the room's pitch — corkage terms themselves aren't sourced here and need a direct question (see Gaps).

## Walkthrough checklist

1. Walk the full guest route — entrance to private room to exit — and time it; a 14-seat exec dinner can't have guests threading through a public dining room.
2. Confirm the room genuinely seats 14 at one table, not 14 split across two tables or 14 "with a squeeze."
3. Check cloakroom and toilet proximity and standard — do guests have to cross the main restaurant floor.
4. Test acoustics with the room empty vs. imagining it full — private rooms often bounce sound with hard surfaces.
5. Check lighting is adjustable and has no aggressive branding/screensaver defaults if AV kit is present (relevant at Black Penny, CORD, Space14-style rooms).
6. Confirm mobile signal inside the room, not just wifi — basement/lower-ground rooms (Black Penny's Seminar Pit, CORD's Viceroy, Vivat Bacchus) are the ones to actually test.
7. Ask to see the room set for dinner, not for the daytime meeting configuration it's often photographed in.
8. Confirm exclusivity — is the door closable, or is it a "private-ish" alcove off a shared space.
9. Check power outlet locations if any AV/presentation use is planned.
10. Confirm supplier/loading access and timing if externally sourced wine or gifts are being brought in.

## Gaps

- Exact seated capacity at Corrigan's Mayfair and 1 Lombard Street's 1776 room — neither source states a number for 14.
- Whether a separate room hire fee applies anywhere other than Bentley's, where it's explicitly waived.
- Actual corkage policy and rate — no source in this batch addresses it beyond CORD's general "extensive wine list" line.
- Whether Vivat Bacchus's Stellenbosch Room (seats 12) can flex to 14 or must combine with an adjacent room, and at what cost.
- Whether Black Penny's Seminar Pit or CORD's Viceroy can be dressed down from their meeting/AV-equipped default into a dinner-only feel — the sources pitch both rooms corporate-meeting-first.
- Current availability for a specific date, and whether any of these rooms are mid-refurbishment or under different management than the source pages describe (all venue pages are undated marketing copy).
