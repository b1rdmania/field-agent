## Negotiation notes

- **Minimum spend**: The cited range for a 14-seat private room is £500–£2,500. Budget or off-peak rooms can run as low as £250–£500. Top-tier or weekend slots can exceed £5,000. If a venue quotes well outside that band, ask what's actually included.
- **Concrete anchor point**: CORD quotes its Viceroy room at "from £1,500" for an afternoon session. Use this figure as a reference when a venue won't give a number up front.
- **Room fee vs. spend**: Bentley's charges no separate room hire fee. The whole cost sits inside the food and drink minimum spend. Ask every venue on this list whether they charge a separate hire fee on top of minimum spend. That's the first thing to trade away.
- **Midweek/off-peak**: The cited answer confirms lower minimums on weekday slots or lunch, versus peak evening slots. For a Thursday executive dinner, push on this instead of accepting a rate built for Friday or Saturday.
- **Wine/corkage**: The cited answers and venue text don't cover this, except that CORD includes an "extensive wine list" as part of the room's pitch. Corkage terms aren't sourced here. Ask about them directly (see Gaps).

## Walkthrough checklist

1. Walk the full guest route from entrance to private room to exit, and time it. A 14-seat executive dinner can't route guests through a public dining room.
2. Confirm the room seats 14 at one table. Check it isn't 14 split across two tables, or 14 with a squeeze.
3. Check the cloakroom and toilet proximity and standard. Confirm guests don't have to cross the main restaurant floor.
4. Test the acoustics with the room empty, then picture it full. Private rooms often bounce sound off hard surfaces.
5. Check the lighting is adjustable. If AV kit is present, check it has no aggressive branding or screensaver defaults. This matters at Black Penny, CORD, and Space14-style rooms.
6. Confirm mobile signal inside the room, not just wifi. Test this at basement or lower-ground rooms: Black Penny's Seminar Pit, CORD's Viceroy, and Vivat Bacchus.
7. Ask to see the room set for dinner. Venues often photograph these rooms in their daytime meeting configuration instead.
8. Confirm exclusivity. Check whether the door closes fully, or whether it's a "private-ish" alcove off a shared space.
9. Check the power outlet locations if you plan any AV or presentation use.
10. Confirm supplier and loading access and timing if you plan to bring in externally sourced wine or gifts.
