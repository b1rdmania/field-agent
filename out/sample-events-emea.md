## The read

The set's EMEA presence is thin and format-light compared to what the same companies do in the US. Tavily runs a developer-community-and-booth playbook in the US (NYU Hackathon, Elastic meetups, LangChain Interrupt sponsorship, Composio dinner). It has only partially exported that playbook to Europe: a Munich partnership, a Prague meetup, one Amsterdam exhibit slot. Brave is the most visibly "enterprise-conference" of the set in EMEA. It appears as a paid sponsor at The AI Summit London and MAD//FEST. This looks like a deliberate advertising and privacy-brand play, not a developer-API go-to-market. That fits Brave's broader ad-supported business, not just its Search API. Perplexity's single EMEA touch, the Billion Pound Build at London Tech Week, is a marquee-moment stunt, not a sustained programme. This research found no other EMEA sponsorship by Perplexity. Firecrawl, You.com, and Parallel show zero EMEA footprint in what this research turned up. This is despite all three running visible US event programmes: Firecrawl's hackathons and TDC São Paulo, You.com's NYC dinner series and Tech Week hosting. Take that gap at face value rather than assuming under-research. It suggests EMEA field marketing in this category is still a secondary motion for most of the set. Activity is built opportunistically around whichever flagship week, such as London Tech Week or MWC-adjacent events, is already drawing the buyer.

The white space is broad. No one in the set runs a developer-dinner or small-room executive format in EMEA the way You.com and Tavily do in the US. The "curated dinner" formats that recur across this research, such as World Summit AI's executive dinner series, IDC & SAP dinners, and HTEC's AI First series, are run by adjacent vendors and analyst firms, not by any search or crawl competitor. Paris (RAISE Summit, VivaTech) and Amsterdam (AI & Big Data Expo, World Summit AI) both show confirmed enterprise-buyer audiences with no competitor from this set present. RAISE Summit in particular looks like an open lane given its NVIDIA, Oracle, OpenAI, and Anthropic vendor mix. Berlin and Munich developer-community events are Tavily's territory alone. No one else in the set shows up there. This reading is drawn from absence, not confirmed intent. Competitors may be present at these events in ways this research did not surface, such as unbadged attendees rather than sponsors. Treat the "nobody's there" read as provisional pending a deeper pass.

## Gaps

- This research is built almost entirely from LinkedIn posts and a handful of event-partner pages. It will systematically miss unannounced attendance, such as badge-scan-only attendance with no sponsor tier and no social post, for every competitor in the set.
- This research found no confirmation either way on whether Firecrawl, You.com, or Parallel attended any EMEA event as an unsponsored attendee rather than a sponsor or host. The absence of a marketing post is not proof of absence from the room.
- Exact dates are missing for the Munich {Tech: Europe} event and MAD//FEST London. This needs direct event-page confirmation.
- This research could not confirm whether the "Parallel" hits (GLOBALS, European Blockchain Convention, CIGRE) refer to the search company Parallel or to a different "Parallel" brand. This needs a targeted search on the specific entity (Parallel Web Systems / parallel.ai) plus a check of its own site and LinkedIn for an events page.
- Buyer-audience quality for several EMEA events (HumanX Amsterdam, {Tech: Europe} Munich, On Ring Lisbon) is taken from the event's own self-description, not an independent attendee breakdown. This is worth verifying with past-year attendee lists where available.
- This research found no pricing or scale data on what sponsorship tiers Brave or Tavily bought, such as a booth versus logo-only versus a speaking slot. This matters for budgeting a comparable Exa presence.
- This research does not cover 2025 EMEA history. It gives no view on whether this set's EMEA presence is growing, shrinking, or holding steady year over year. A follow-up pull scoped to 2025 would give trend context.
