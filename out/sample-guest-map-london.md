## Account briefs

**Revolut**: Live signal: strong. [redacted], Group Head of ML Engineering, spoke publicly at RAAIS 2026 about running AI at scale inside a regulated bank. He said the hard problem has moved from the model to the surrounding control plane. That control plane covers the gateway, the governance layer, fallback measurement, cost controls, and human review layers. It wraps around an AI assistant called AIR. AIR sits in the path of over 1 trillion dollars of transactions across 200+ products. Revolut is actively wrestling with retrieval, grounding, governance, and reliability at the infrastructure layer. This is the exact conversation Exa's API is built for. Engineering leadership is also in flux. Revolut promoted [redacted] into a new Group-level ML role in April 2026. Revolut separately hired [redacted] as Head of Engineering, Retail. [redacted] frames their remit as building AI-enabled products and platforms. The timing matters because Revolut has a named architectural gap in its control plane and governance. Revolut also has a fresh leadership mandate to fill that gap.

**Monzo**: Live signal: moderate, real but earlier-stage. A June 2026 engineering blog post titled "Engineering the Future of Customer Operations: The Monzo Ops Agent" states that Monzo has recently begun exploring how generative AI can improve the customer experience. Monzo already uses classical ML in fraud, credit, and personalisation. Its initial generative AI focus is customer support and fraud and financial-crime investigation workflows. Both areas depend on pulling together customer context, product knowledge, and real-time context reliably in a regulated environment. This effort is genuinely early. Monzo itself flags reliability and validation standards as the open problem. That makes it a good moment to shape Monzo's retrieval approach before it sets. Monzo's live signal is less mature than Revolut's or Checkout's live builds.

**Checkout.com**: Live signal: strong and concrete. Checkout.com published two engineering blog posts in mid-2026. The first, from June 2026, is titled "How Agent HAL has become a core part of how Checkout.com ships software." It reports that Checkout.com's in-house AI coding agent generates 18% of all pull requests. The agent is deliberately scoped to narrow, bounded tasks embedded in existing process, not flashy zero-to-one generation. The second post, from July 2026, is titled "Operationalizing AI in Engineering and beyond." It frames AI as core to Checkout.com's SDLC performance culture, under the banner "No Room for Approximation." Checkout.com also has a dedicated Data & AI Engineering platform team. That team builds internal AI infrastructure using LangChain, LangGraph, and stream processing. The timing matters because Checkout.com already ships agentic tooling in production. Checkout.com also has a data and AI platform team actively building the plumbing underneath it. This is a direct fit for a search and retrieval infrastructure conversation.
