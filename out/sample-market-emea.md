## What this run suggests

**On the base question, the evidence points to London.** It has the deepest cross-industry buyer bench, the densest AI-native anchor calendar, and format norms that all line up. London is the natural seat for an EMEA operation. It is also the natural first events market.

**On events focus, London should take the quarter's primary investment.** Build it around a hosted dinner timed near an existing anchor event, either AI Summit London or AI Native DevCon, both in June. Skip a standalone booth. The research shows the deepest bench of senior, cross-industry AI and cloud procurement titles, at GSK, Microsoft, and Dyson. The market has also moved toward fewer, higher-spend, purpose-led events. A dinner for 15 to 20 named accounts converts better here than a crowded expo floor. It also gives sales a tight target list to work into pipeline before and after the event.

**Amsterdam gets the second slot, through a roundtable tied to AGNTCon+MCPCon Europe in September.** This conference focuses specifically on agentic AI infrastructure. It is the single most on-thesis conference in the entire dataset for a search or platform vendor. The buyer evidence here is narrower than London's, since it concentrates in Booking.com and ING. The format should be a smaller, curated roundtable that rides the conference's existing draw. Do not fund a standalone event. This keeps cost proportional to the evidence.

Hold Munich and Paris this quarter. Munich's buyer signal is real, but it really comes from one account, BMW, plus Munich Re. This calls for an account-based-marketing motion that sales should run directly. It is not a city-level field investment. Paris has scale through VivaTech and Devoxx, but its buyer-title evidence for AI-platform purchasing is the thinnest in the set. Make a French move once we have a named target list, not ahead of one.

## What would change this call

- Munich: a second and third named enterprise AI-platform buyer outside BMW and Munich Re would upgrade Munich from an account play to a market play.
- Paris: direct evidence of AI-platform procurement titles, not general IT or software titles, attending VivaTech, Devoxx, or dotAI would strengthen Paris's ranking. Confirmation of Dutch corporate-event norms would do the same for Amsterdam. Both rankings could then move with confidence instead of caveats.
- Amsterdam: a second account beyond Booking.com and ING, with a named AI-buying title, would close the density gap with London. This could bump Amsterdam to the top spot, given its stronger topical conference fit.
