## Who owns what

- **Grounding — Microsoft, firmly.** Not a one-off mention: Microsoft has a named product (Web IQ, launched at Build, 2 Jun 2026), a named architecture concept used by a third-party framework (Jason Barnard's "ARGDW" pipeline, with a Microsoft PM giving a dedicated session on the "Grounded gate" at Kalicube Summit, 24 Jun 2026), and an internal engineering blog series (Command Line) walking through the grounding stack. That's a recurring format plus a shipped product carrying the word, not just marketing copy — this is the firmest claim in the research.
- **Agentic relevance / "let the agents do the engineering" — OpenSearch/AWS, moderately.** "Agentic Relevance Tuning" (ART) was introduced as a named framework at OpenSearchCon Europe 2026 by AWS and OpenSource Connections, and the conference's own keynote (Lewis/Curtis) plus a companion Haystack talk both used agentic framing for search infra. Two named instances in one season across two overlapping communities (OpenSearchCon, Haystack) is a real cluster, but it's still one conference season, not yet a multi-year recurring slot.
- **Deep research agents — contested, nobody has annexed it yet.** Google (Deep Research/Deep Research Max), Exa (Exa Agent), and Firecrawl (deep research for AI agents blog) are all using the phrase within weeks of each other in 2026, plus an independent conference talk (Thoughtworks at Arc of AI). This is a phrase with real product weight behind it, but ownership is split three-to-four ways — nobody has a named recurring format claiming it. Track, don't cede.
- **Context engineering — Elastic, tentatively.** One named speaker (Kathleen DeRusso), one dated main-stage slot (HaystackConf, 7 May 2026), explicitly framed as an "evolution of relevance engineering to context engineering." That is a real, dated, credible instance — but it is one talk from one practitioner, not yet a recurring format. Watch whether Elastic repeats this at the next Haystack or OpenSearchCon before treating it as owned.
- **"Window to the web your agents can trust" — Tavily, on brand voice only.** Recurring paid campaign (third out-of-home/bus-ad run per VP Marketing's own post), consistent phrase across campaigns. This is owned as a brand line, not as a category term with buyer pull — it's marketing repetition, not a stage or format.

## Unclaimed

- **The vector-search correction / "hybrid is what actually works" story.** Evidence of demand: TechTarget, Dell, Pureinsights, and AAXIS all independently published operator-facing pieces in 2026 making the same point — vector-only deployments break down at scale, hybrid retrieval is what production teams actually ship, and the "DIY stack" burden (governance, cost, integration) is what practitioners complain about, not model choice. This is trade-press and vendor-blog consensus, not a single stage moment — nobody has built a keynote or named framework specifically around "the hybrid correction" itself; it shows up inside other talks (Couldwell, Uchoa) as a supporting point, never the headline. For this to carry a room rather than a panel, someone would need operator case-study data (not vendor blog claims) showing cost/latency numbers from a name-brand company's migration off vector-only — the Rabobank talk at QCon London (Lan Chu) is the closest thing in this research to that shape, and it's about RAG production lessons generally, not framed as an anti-vector-hype talk.
- **Agent-native search APIs as a distinct infrastructure layer (Exa/Tavily/Firecrawl/Brave/Serper category).** Evidence of demand: Max Buckley's own GenAI Zürich framing is the sharpest signal in the research — he explicitly argues Microsoft and Google are *ceding* this space by bundling grounding tightly into their own agent stacks (Bing Search API retired Aug 2025; Google's web access only via Gemini grounding), leaving room for independent players. Tavily's hiring/funding growth (95 people, +280% YoY, $70M raised) and its paid out-of-home campaigns are demand signals from the vendor side, but there is no independent conference or analyst stage in this research treating "agent-native search API" as a named category the way "grounding" or "hybrid retrieval" have stages. For this to carry a room: an operator (not a vendor) needs to stand up and compare providers on production numbers — cost per call, latency, agent task success rate — the way Web IQ's "sub-165ms P95" claim was made publicly comparable. Nobody has done that yet in what's here.
- **Data/context readiness as the actual adoption bottleneck.** Evidence of demand: this is the single most repeated operator complaint across sources — S&P Global's Jim Curtis cited 35% of enterprises citing insufficient data access as a blocker at OpenSearchCon; Dell's blog says teams spend ~80% of time on data prep and reason over <30% of their data; multiple pieces (AAXIS, Pivot News, Semantic.io) treat governance/lifecycle/observability as the unglamorous real work. It's consistently the subtext of keynotes (Lewis/Curtis explicitly named it "the bottleneck has moved from the model to the data") but never the headline topic of a talk in this research — it's the thing panels gesture at, not the thing panels are about. For it to carry a room: someone would need to make the boring data-layer problem the explicit subject, probably paired with hard numbers from one enterprise, rather than a caveat inside a search-relevance talk.

## The read

The language that's actually moving is the reframing of search infrastructure around agents as the consumer, not humans — "grounding" has gone from a vague RAG buzzword to a named, shipped product layer (Microsoft's Web IQ, Google's Gemini grounding), and "context engineering" is being explicitly positioned by at least one credible practitioner (Elastic's DeRusso) as the successor discipline to relevance engineering. That's a real shift in vocabulary tracking a real shift in who the buyer is: the OpenSearchCon keynote's "insight, not just search results" framing and the repeated "80% of time on data prep" complaint both point at the same underlying buyer pain — production teams are stuck on data and retrieval quality, not on the model layer, and the words on stage (grounding, hybrid, agentic relevance) are following that pain rather than leading it.

The honest risk is that "deep research agents" and "agentic search" specifically are running ahead of buyer evidence. Both are heavily represented by vendor product launches and vendor blogs (Google, Exa, Firecrawl, You.com, Tavily) using near-identical language ("connect agents to the live web," "the missing infrastructure for the agentic era," "highways of the agentic era") within the same few months of 2026 — that's a strong tell of vendors converging on shared marketing language rather than buyers demanding a named category. The one operator-side talk that touches this directly (Thoughtworks at Arc of AI) is about production lessons, not endorsing the category name. This research cannot separate "buyers are asking for deep research agents" from "vendors decided this is what they're all calling the same underlying capability this quarter" — say that plainly rather than picking a side.
