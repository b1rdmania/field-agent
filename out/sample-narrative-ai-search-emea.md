## Who owns what

- **Grounding — Microsoft, firmly.** This is not a one-off mention. Microsoft has a named product: Web IQ, launched at Build on 2 Jun 2026. A third-party framework uses a named architecture concept built on it: Jason Barnard's "ARGDW" pipeline. A Microsoft PM gave a dedicated session on the "Grounded gate" at Kalicube Summit on 24 Jun 2026. Microsoft's internal engineering blog (Command Line) walks through the grounding stack in a series. This is a recurring format plus a shipped product carrying the word. It is the firmest claim in the research.
- **Agentic relevance / "let the agents do the engineering" — OpenSearch/AWS, moderately.** AWS and OpenSource Connections introduced "Agentic Relevance Tuning" (ART) as a named framework at OpenSearchCon Europe 2026. The conference's own keynote (Lewis/Curtis) and a companion Haystack talk both used agentic framing for search infrastructure. Two named instances in one season across two overlapping communities is a real cluster. But it is still one conference season, not yet a multi-year recurring slot.
- **Deep research agents — contested, nobody has claimed it yet.** Google (Deep Research/Deep Research Max), Exa (Exa Agent), and Firecrawl (deep research for AI agents blog) all used the phrase within weeks of each other in 2026. An independent conference talk (Thoughtworks at Arc of AI) covered the same ground. This is a phrase with real product weight behind it. But ownership splits three to four ways. Nobody has a named recurring format claiming it. Track this, do not cede it.
- **Context engineering — Elastic, tentatively.** One named speaker, Kathleen DeRusso, gave one dated main-stage talk at HaystackConf on 7 May 2026. She framed it explicitly as an "evolution of relevance engineering to context engineering." That is a real, dated, credible instance. But it is one talk from one practitioner, not yet a recurring format. Watch whether Elastic repeats this at the next Haystack or OpenSearchCon before treating it as owned.
- **"Window to the web your agents can trust" — Tavily, on brand voice only.** This is a recurring paid campaign. It is the third out-of-home/bus-ad run, per the VP Marketing's own post. The phrase stays consistent across campaigns. This is owned as a brand line, not as a category term with buyer pull. It is marketing repetition, not a stage or format.

## Unclaimed

- **The vector-search correction / "hybrid is what actually works" story.** TechTarget, Dell, Pureinsights, and AAXIS each independently published operator-facing pieces in 2026 making the same point. Vector-only deployments break down at scale. Hybrid retrieval is what production teams actually ship. Practitioners complain about the "DIY stack" burden — governance, cost, integration — not about model choice. This is trade-press and vendor-blog consensus, not a single stage moment. Nobody has built a keynote or named framework around "the hybrid correction" itself. It shows up inside other talks (Couldwell, Uchoa) as a supporting point, never as the headline. To carry a room rather than a panel, someone needs operator case-study data, not vendor blog claims. That data should show cost and latency numbers from a name-brand company's migration off vector-only. The Rabobank talk at QCon London (Lan Chu) comes closest to that shape in this research. But it covers RAG production lessons generally. It is not framed as an anti-vector-hype talk.
- **Agent-native search APIs as a distinct infrastructure layer (Exa/Tavily/Firecrawl/Brave/Serper category).** Max Buckley's own GenAI Zürich framing is the sharpest signal in the research. He argues that Microsoft and Google are ceding this space by bundling grounding tightly into their own agent stacks. Microsoft retired the Bing Search API in Aug 2025. Google offers web access only through Gemini grounding. This leaves room for independent players. Tavily's hiring and funding growth are demand signals from the vendor side: 95 people, up 280% year over year, and $70M raised. Its paid out-of-home campaigns are also vendor-side signals. But no independent conference or analyst stage in this research treats "agent-native search API" as a named category, the way "grounding" or "hybrid retrieval" have stages. To carry a room, an operator, not a vendor, needs to compare providers on production numbers: cost per call, latency, and agent task success rate. Web IQ's "sub-165ms P95" claim made that kind of comparison public. Nobody has done that yet for this category.
- **Data/context readiness as the actual adoption bottleneck.** This is the single most repeated operator complaint across sources. S&P Global's Jim Curtis cited 35% of enterprises naming insufficient data access as a blocker, at OpenSearchCon. Dell's blog says teams spend about 80% of their time on data prep and reason over less than 30% of their data. Multiple pieces (AAXIS, Pivot News, Semantic.io) treat governance, lifecycle, and observability as the unglamorous real work. It surfaces as the subtext of keynotes. Lewis and Curtis explicitly named it "the bottleneck has moved from the model to the data." But it is never the headline topic of a talk in this research. Panels gesture at it; no panel is about it. To carry a room, someone needs to make the data-layer problem the explicit subject, paired with hard numbers from one enterprise, rather than a caveat inside a search-relevance talk.

## The read

The language that is actually moving reframes search infrastructure around agents as the consumer, not humans. "Grounding" has gone from a vague RAG buzzword to a named, shipped product layer: Microsoft's Web IQ, Google's Gemini grounding. At least one credible practitioner, Elastic's DeRusso, explicitly positions "context engineering" as the successor discipline to relevance engineering. This is a real shift in vocabulary tracking a real shift in who the buyer is. The OpenSearchCon keynote framed its goal as "insight, not just search results." The repeated complaint that teams spend 80% of their time on data prep points at the same underlying buyer pain. Production teams are stuck on data and retrieval quality, not on the model layer. The words on stage — grounding, hybrid, agentic relevance — are following that pain, not leading it.

The honest risk is that "deep research agents" and "agentic search" are running ahead of buyer evidence. Vendor product launches and vendor blogs heavily represent both terms: Google, Exa, Firecrawl, You.com, and Tavily all used near-identical language — "connect agents to the live web," "the missing infrastructure for the agentic era," "highways of the agentic era" — within the same few months of 2026. That is a strong tell of vendors converging on shared marketing language, not of buyers demanding a named category. The one operator-side talk that touches this directly, Thoughtworks at Arc of AI, covers production lessons. It does not endorse the category name. This research cannot separate "buyers are asking for deep research agents" from "vendors decided this is what they are all calling the same underlying capability this quarter." State that plainly rather than picking a side.
